# BIE solver novelty: boundary integral equation candidate solver

## Question and search scope

**Verdict: B, a direct extension, for the solver as presently specified.** A standalone numerical-methods paper is defensible if it demonstrates a useful accuracy–cost or robustness advantage for curved dielectric interfaces between different periodic claddings. The present composition, without that comparison, is not a strong methods contribution by itself. I did not locate this exact implementation bundle in the full texts read within the scope below. Its main ingredients, including the physical fixed-$\beta$ frequency problem and different periodic half-leads, are already in the literature.

The closest physical formulation is Fliss–Klindworth–Schmidt (2015); the closest cell-BIE/half-array closure is Petropoulos–Turc (2025); the closest earlier multipole guided-mode construction is Botten and coworkers (2006), building on their 2004 Bloch-interface calculus for different crystals. Agocs–Barnett (2024) is an additional direct warning against claiming that fixed-quasimomentum trapped-mode frequencies found by a BIE determinant and singular-value decomposition (SVD) are a new algorithmic principle.

Search cutoff: **7 November 2026**. I used the July audit as a starting list, then read the present method and numerical records rather than transferring its grades. The method section named in the request is now `draft/bie-solver/sec-method.tex`; its cell representation is explicitly an ansatz, and its search matrix explicitly uses $N_\pm D_\pm^{-1}$. The companion enclosure and its interval width are not graded as solver contributions here.

The searches covered web-indexed publisher records, Crossref, arXiv, HAL and institutional/author repositories, with backward and forward citation chasing from Yuan–Lu–Antoine, Botten, Fliss, Barnett–Greengard and recent periodic BIE papers. Query families combined guided/defect/interface modes, fixed frequency or fixed tangential Bloch parameter, dielectric transmission, different photonic crystals, cell DtN/NtD/RtR, multipoles, Bloch scattering pencils, stable/Jordan subspaces, periodization, determinant/Chebyshev/contour/AAA eigenvalue search, and 2020–2026 periodic-waveguide BIE work. The named Lu, Botten, Fliss/Joly/Schmidt/Kappeler, Zhang/Hu, Barnett/Cho/Agocs, Bruno, Tausch and Pérez-Arancibia groups were included. I did not conduct a subscription Scopus or Web of Science search. “Not located” below refers to this scope, not an exhaustive priority claim.

PDF page numbers below refer to the specific public/preprint or local-library version identified in the row; printed journal pages are used where available. A tolerance, a self-convergence observation and an independently known eigenvalue error are kept distinct.

## Source table

Here “different leads” means different periodic materials or cells, not simply two ends of the same guide. “Allowed” distinguishes model scope from a demonstrated numerical example. BIE = boundary integral equation; DtN/NtD = Dirichlet-to-Neumann/Neumann-to-Dirichlet; RtR = Robin-to-Robin; NEP = nonlinear eigenvalue problem; QZ = generalized Schur decomposition. FEM = finite element method; QP = quasi-periodic. AAA denotes the adaptive barycentric rational-approximation algorithm used in the cited root searches. Frequency notation varies between sources; the column identifies the physical quantity rather than copying their symbol $k$ or $\beta$.

The exact differences for the three closest published compositions are:

1. **Fliss–Klindworth–Schmidt:** essentially the same dielectric line-defect/fixed-tangential-phase frequency eigenproblem, including different claddings, but FEM with RtR/Riccati closure rather than Müller cell BIE, ordered-QZ port spaces and a DtN-graph determinant.
2. **Petropoulos–Turc:** BIE cell RtR plus decaying generalized-pencil/Riccati half-array closure and central defects, but identical sound-soft arrays with fixed-frequency forcing rather than a dielectric transmission strip with fixed phase and an unknown guided frequency.
3. **Botten et al. (2006):** scalar dielectric line-guide modes are already roots of $\det Z(k,\alpha_0)=0$ with fixed tangential phase and infinite Bloch-reflection claddings; the displayed construction uses circular-cylinder multipoles/fictitious-source responses in one bulk crystal rather than Müller cell BIEs with independently different claddings and whole stable-QZ spaces. Their 2004 interface calculus separately covers different crystal regions.

## Proposed contribution statement and claims to avoid

**Proposed contribution statement.** We develop and evaluate a boundary-only solver for fixed-tangential-Bloch guided-mode frequencies in two-dimensional dielectric line defects bounded by different periodic claddings. High-order transmission cell solves provide Fourier-port scattering matrices, and ordered generalized Schur subspaces represent both semi-infinite claddings at the discrete level without a finite-depth supercell truncation. Eliminating the central-cell densities leaves a small frequency-dependent port matrix, whose candidate roots are localized by determinant interpolation and checked by singular values and field reconstruction. The contribution is the implementation and quantified performance of this combination for curved inclusions, unequal normal periods, asymmetric cells and weak confinement, measured against an independently converged transparent-boundary FEM solver and a boundary-based reference on an overlapping test geometry. A separate enclosure calculation may supply reference spectral information, but the candidate solver itself claims no error bound or complete root count.

The performance and comparison clauses are targets for the paper, not results established by this survey. If they cannot be demonstrated, the defensible output is a solver implementation accompanying the enclosure paper rather than a separate strong methods claim.

Claims to avoid:

- A new exact reduction of a line-defect eigenproblem to one cell; new treatment of fixed $\beta$; or new permission for two different periodic claddings. Fliss and coworkers already cover these.
- A new BIE periodization, Bloch scattering principle, generalized-mode theory, or determinant/Chebyshev eigensolver.
- Pole-free root search merely because the decaying space was initially defined without a DtN inverse. The implemented scalar search reconstructs $N_\pm D_\pm^{-1}$; RtR-based prior work explicitly studies the resulting exceptional-frequency problem.
- A proven complete or unique cell representation, spectral convergence for arbitrary $C^2$ boundaries, or recovery of every mode from a sampled coefficient-decay test. These require additional analysis or narrower assumptions.
- Solver error equal to the companion's enclosure width, root-polishing increment, or smallest-singular-value ratio. They measure different things.
- Candidate-solver speedup inferred from a comparison with the FEM isolation/counting/enclosure chain. Compare equal solver tasks and equal accuracy; keep enclosure work separate.

## Open questions

1. Obtain lawful full texts of N14, N15 and N17 before a submission-level priority statement. In particular, Lyu's bi-infinite interface eigenproblem could strengthen the required numerical baseline even if its FEM/cyclic-doubling discretization differs. Its publisher introduction is insufficient to compare accuracy and cost. White's applications paper must be checked using the correct document, not the mismatched attachment.
2. Agocs–Goodwill–Hoskins must be included when positioning BIE junctions of different periodic media. Its grating geometry and fixed-frequency scattering differ from the present strip eigenproblem.
3. The current method source calls the augmented center representation an ansatz. A solver-only paper cannot use the companion enclosure's independence from that ansatz as a substitute for explaining when candidates represent physical modes and how representation singularities are detected. A stronger continuous equivalence or convergence claim needs its own analysis.
4. The appropriate practical contribution is still undecided until E1/E2. A common gap and a Fourier-port dimension of $2K$ do not by themselves imply lower computational cost than existing transparent-boundary or Bloch-scattering solvers. Assembly, stable-subspace construction, nonlinear search and field recovery all belong in that cost.
5. The observed root plateau at about $10^{-14}$ is a discrete-search observation. The companion's $\mu$ interval width about $2.7\times10^{-11}$ is separate spectral information; neither shows that every requested root digit is accurate for the continuous problem. The paper should state a realistic target accuracy and provide an independent reference at that target.
6. The broader Tausch–Butler DtN papers cited in Tausch (2013) remain useful older citation-chasing leads. They are not used here to support unverified theorem or accuracy claims. No exact S1–S8 match was located in the full texts read, and the access gaps prevent a universal nonexistence statement.
