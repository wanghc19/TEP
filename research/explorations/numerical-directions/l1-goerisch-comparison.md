# L1 and the Goerisch route

**Verdict (c): combine them.** L1 means research line 1, the guaranteed enclosure of a finite element method (FEM) plus Dirichlet-to-Neumann (DtN) guided-mode candidate. Retain sketch steps 1–2 to construct the finite global field $w_d$ and reuse step 4's mass/energy integrals. Use **$w_d$ itself as the Goerisch trial**, replacing steps 3, 5 and 6 by its flux/Gram enclosure. This removes the separate quotient-transfer budget that dominates L1's projected interval. Goerisch applied directly to a tapered FEM field is the comparison baseline. The recommendation reuses an implemented, accurate trial; its final width and cost remain unmeasured.

**1. What Goerisch needs on this strip.** Write $\beta_0=0.5$ for the transverse phase and $(\alpha_-,\alpha_+)$ for the sketch's gap interval $(\alpha,\beta)$, avoiding its double use of $\beta$. On $B=\mathbb R\times(-1/2,1/2)$, $1\le\rho\le17$, set $V=H^1_{\beta_0}(B)$, $a(v,z)=\int_B\nabla v\cdot\overline{\nabla z}$, $b(v,z)=\int_B\rho v\bar z$, $\|v\|_\rho^2=b(v,v)$, and $a_\gamma=a+\gamma b$ with $\gamma>0$. The book's associated operator acts in this energy Hilbert space (Eqs. (10.47), (10.50), pp.373–374); it is unitarily equivalent to $A+\gamma$ through $(A+\gamma)^{1/2}$. For a trial $v\in V$, its implicit source solution $z$ satisfies
$$a_\gamma(z,\psi)=b(v,\psi)\quad(\psi\in V),\qquad -\Delta z+\gamma\rho z=\rho v.$$
This is Eq. (10.52), p.375, specialized as in Example 10.27, Eq. (10.74), p.382. The book treats Dirichlet boundaries; the following quasiperiodic adaptation follows by cancellation of the paired boundary terms. In Lemma 10.25's notation, distinguish its auxiliary form $b_X$ from the physical mass form $b$:
$$X=L^2(B)^2\times L^2(B),\quad b_X((p,r),(p',r'))=\int_B(p\cdot\overline{p'}+\gamma\rho r\overline{r'}),\quad Tv=(\nabla v,v).$$
Then $b_X(Tv,T\psi)=a_\gamma(v,\psi)$. For any $p\in H(\operatorname{div},B)$ with traces $p_y(x,1/2)=e^{\mathrm i\beta_0}p_y(x,-1/2)$, take
$$\hat w=\left(p,\frac{\operatorname{div}p+\rho v}{\gamma\rho}\right),\qquad b_X(\hat w,T\psi)=b(v,\psi)\quad(\psi\in V).$$
This is exactly the admissibility equation (10.69), Lemma 10.25, p.380, with the flux construction (10.75)–(10.77), Example 10.27, p.383. The scalar component absorbs the divergence defect; a flux solve need only be approximate.

For the half-leads, use the existing finite-cell FEM extension, enforce matching value traces and the exact phase, and multiply by a Lipschitz taper vanishing at the outer cuts. Its zero extension is a global $V$ trial. Use Raviart–Thomas fluxes on these cells with matching normal traces, the transverse phase, and zero normal trace at the outer cuts; extend the flux by zero. This explicitly constructs both objects on the infinite strip and pays taper error in their quality. Alternatively, retain decaying lead extensions and enclose their tail integrals. An exact physical half-lead solution is unnecessary. The current hard-cut $L^2$ input is inadmissible as $v$ unless its cut traces vanish; $w_d$ itself is another admissible choice. Example 10.38, pp.404–406, demonstrates FEM/flux waveguide bounds through finite-domain comparisons below a symmetry-reduced essential threshold; it supplies no periodic internal-gap tail implementation.

**2. Goerisch order — derived here.** Normalize $b(v,v)=1$ and put $q=a_\gamma(v,v)$, $G=a_\gamma(z,z)$ and $J=b_X(\hat w,\hat w)$. Lemma 10.25's proof, p.380, gives the exact identity
$$J=G+\delta_F^2,\qquad \delta_F^2=\|p-\nabla z\|_0^2+\int_B\frac{|\operatorname{div}(p-\nabla z)|^2}{\gamma\rho}.$$
For $N=1$, Eq. (10.54), p.375, and Theorem 10.29, Eqs. (10.81)–(10.87), pp.386–387, give $A_1=1$, $A_2=q$, $\hat A_0=J$. With $\mu=\alpha_-+\gamma$, $\nu=\alpha_++\gamma$, define
$$\kappa(t)=\frac{1-tJ}{q-2t+t^2J},\qquad E(t)=t+\kappa(t)^{-1}-\gamma=\frac{q-t}{1-tJ}-\gamma.$$
Subject to the theorem's spectral conditions and $\kappa(\mu)>1/(\nu-\mu)$, $\kappa(\nu)<-1/(\nu-\mu)$, with $q-2t+t^2J>0$ at both endpoints, the enclosure is $[E(\nu),E(\mu)]$. Subtraction yields
$$W_G=\frac{(\nu-\mu)(qJ-1)}{(1-\mu J)(\nu J-1)},\qquad qJ-1=(qG-1)+q\delta_F^2.$$
To identify the field order, let $\phi$ be the normalized exact eigenfunction, $e_v=v-\phi$, $\ell=\lambda+\gamma$ and $\epsilon_V=\|e_v\|_{a_\gamma}$. Expansion and $b(v,v)=b(\phi,\phi)=1$ give
$$q-\ell=\|e_v\|_{a_\gamma}^2-\ell b(e_v,e_v),\qquad G-\ell^{-1}=b(e_v,(A+\gamma)^{-1}e_v)-\ell^{-1}b(e_v,e_v).$$
Both differences are $O(\epsilon_V^2)$, so $W_G=O(\epsilon_V^2+\delta_F^2)$ when the gap distances stay positive. Quadrature/rounding uncertainty in the Gram entries adds its own error budget. This is a derivation from the scalar formula, rather than an asserted convergence theorem for a particular flux space.

**L1 order — Kato–Temple cited, transfer order derived.** Theorem 10.8, pp.356–357, gives the exact-quotient width $\eta^2[(\theta-\alpha_-)^{-1}+(\alpha_+-\theta)^{-1}]$, with $\theta=\theta(w)$ and $\eta=\|(A-\theta)w\|_\rho/\|w\|_\rho$. Sketch Proposition 2 adds $O(E_s^2+\eta E_0)$, where $E_s$ and $E_0$ bound $\|w-w_d\|_{L_s}/\|w_d\|_\rho$ and $\|w-w_d\|_\rho/\|w_d\|_\rho$, respectively. Here $\|e\|_{L_s}^2=\|\nabla e\|_0^2+s\|e\|_0^2$; its control of $\|e\|_\rho^2$ absorbs the remaining quadratic mass term. By I9.6 Eq. (7), boundedness of $L_s^{-1}$, and $L_s\phi=(\lambda\rho+s)\phi$, $\eta=O(\|u-\phi\|_0+|\mu_0-\lambda|)$ when the normalization stays bounded away from zero. Thus both routes are quadratic in their stated field-error norms, which differ. Neither order alone proves bounded effectivity against the actual eigenvalue error inside a gap.

**3. Gap information.** For both parts of Theorem 10.29, pp.386–388, the shifted interval must contain no essential spectrum; the respective accumulation and endpoint-eigenvector conditions must hold. In our standing common-essential-gap setting, A1 and the scalar checks above provide the same isolation/existence information needed by L1. Positive shifted endpoints are arranged by $\gamma$. Without uniqueness, the two Goerisch bounds refer to the smallest and largest eigenvalues separately. Goerisch removes neither lead-gap verification nor the need to identify one spectral point. Its source/flux equations remain coercive irrespective of the target's position within the gap.

**4. Cost.** The full Goerisch route adds a global conforming trial and a finite flux minimization of $J$; Example 10.27, Eq. (10.77), p.383, explicitly permits an approximately solved linear system. It avoids enclosing the solution of the variable-coefficient source equation. Its integrals contain $\rho$ and $1/\rho$ on the true curved inclusions, and its three Gram entries and scalar signs require interval control. Compact support avoids infinite integration but introduces taper error. L1 already has its source moments and closed-form tails; after moments are available, the forward/backward recurrences cost $O((2M+1)N_x)$ for $N_x$ source columns (I9.6 Eqs. (20)–(23)). Curved-interface source and mass integrals, quotient evaluation, finite-representation errors, and rounding still need certification. No measured cost for the new Goerisch solve is available.

**5. The useful combination and the constant-coefficient distinction.** L1's $L_s$ permits arbitrary $L^2$ input, diagonal Fourier inversion and explicit exponential tails, independent of the two lead materials. Those are real implementation advantages here. They are not exclusive to this framework: since $\min_m|\beta_0+2\pi m|^2=0.25$, $a$ is coercive on $V$. Taking $\gamma=0$ in the general Lemma 10.25 setting, p.380, with $X=L^2(B)^2$ and $Tv=\nabla v$, also gives the constant-coefficient source $-\Delta z=\rho v$, but requires exact equilibrium $-\operatorname{div}p=\rho v$. This follows from the lemma, rather than the positive-reaction specialization of Example 10.27, pp.381–383. A polynomial flux on a mesh cutting the true interfaces needs an additional lifting to meet that identity exactly.

For the recommended combination, set $v=w_d/\|w_d\|_\rho$. I9.6 Eq. (16) and §2 give $w_d\in H^2_{\beta_0}(B)\subset V$, so Theorem 10.29, pp.386–388, accepts it without any $w_d\to w$ transfer. Treat its stored source coefficients as the exact definition of the chosen trial. Source-projection and moment errors then affect trial quality, rather than entering as separate certified transfer penalties. Compute the flux and three Gram entries for unnormalized $w_d$, then divide by its enclosed mass; validate every integral using the true material.

Choose $p$ on a finite box $D$ as above, with zero extension. Outside $D$, admissibility prescribes $\hat w=(0,v/\gamma)$, giving the explicit tail term
$$J_{B\setminus D}=\gamma^{-1}\int_{B\setminus D}\rho|v|^2\le \frac{17T_d^2}{\gamma\|w_d\|_\rho^2},$$
where $T_d^2$ is I9.6's analytic tail norm, Eq. (23). The gradient tail in $a_\gamma$ is also obtained mode by mode. Thus one approximate positive-definite flux minimization and certified body/tail integrals suffice; the optimal variable-coefficient source solution $z$ stays implicit. This gives a concrete answer to “why not just Goerisch?”: Goerisch supplies the enclosure, while L1 supplies a convenient global trial from the current $L^2$ input. A direct tapered-FEM trial could still be cheaper and must be compared. Avoiding a separate transfer penalty does not remove the projection error already present in $v$.

**Reference-value check and open points.** The control figures in sketch §5 are candidate error $5.8\times10^{-7}$, $R_\mu=1.21\times10^{-4}$, projected total width $5.1\times10^{-7}$, and transfer-energy contribution $4.7\times10^{-7}$; the two Kato–Temple sides sum to about $2.7\times10^{-8}$. The ordinary quotient of $w_d$ is only $2.94\times10^{-8}$ above the reference. The sketch estimates roughly 16-fold slack in the dominant transfer term. These figures motivate reusing $w_d$ and testing an enclosure without that separate term; they do not determine its Goerisch width.

For either Goerisch trial, the exact success test is $W_G<5\times10^{-7}$. For illustration only, use $\gamma=1$ and sketch §5's **unverified** edges $\alpha_-=1.3174^2$, $\alpha_+=2.1875^2$, with $\lambda\approx2.428729$. Hand substitution near $J=1/(\lambda+1)$ gives $W_G\approx22[(qG-1)+q\delta_F^2]$: the total bracket must be below about $2.3\times10^{-8}$, with room for interval evaluation. Even with negligible field contribution, this requires $\delta_F\lesssim8\times10^{-5}$ in the normalized flux norm above. Acceptance uses the computable $qJ-1$; separate knowledge of $G$ and $\delta_F$ is unnecessary. The candidate eigenvalue error alone determines neither term. Open: whether either trial and an affordable flux space meet this test, whether the finite-box tail majorant is small enough, their relative cost, and certification of the assumed gap and final integrals. These comparisons require measurements, which this reading-only task does not supply.
