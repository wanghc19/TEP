The target from [threshold uncertainty](/Users/whc/Documents/Work/dot-research/research/explorations/bend-scattering-methods/candidates/threshold-uncertainty.md) is the complex reflection amplitude of an incident channel that stays open, while another channel's continuous cutoff is known only within a numerical interval. The desired guarantee couples cell/cutoff, port, and center errors when their size is comparable to distance from cutoff. The old Neumann-sidewall prototype helps compare methods; any eventual demonstration must return to the repository's transverse-quasiperiodic model.

### Verdict: PARTIAL

Nazarov already supplies a periodic-exit algorithm with scattering-coefficient truncation bounds and critical generalized waves, so a generic claim of reliable threshold truncation would be covered. The narrower target remains the joint guarantee with numerically enclosed cutoff and port data, including computable stability factors and a meaningful reflection interval when cell error and cutoff distance shrink together. Combining existing augmented ports, cell estimates, and perturbation theory is eligible if it supplies that guarantee; the current literature check leaves its cost and usefulness undecided, with the 2015 theorem still an access limitation.
