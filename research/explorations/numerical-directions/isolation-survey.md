# Certifying the isolation assumption (H)

**There are published isolation and exact-count results for internal gaps of operators without compact resolvent, but the inspected sources do not give a ready numerical certificate for our two-dimensional dielectric guide.** Davies–Plum prove isolation for a particular noncompact operator by a scalar spectral equation; Brown and coauthors prove exact mode counts for sufficiently weak positive line defects, giving (H) when the count is one. The closest inspected implemented, verified periodic-gap method is Nagatou–Plum–Nakao's one-dimensional eigenvalue exclusion. It certifies empty intervals, but leaves the number of eigenvalues inside a retained candidate interval unresolved. For our fixed, possibly different periodic leads, the missing step is a computable upper count for the continuous operator. A relevant one-dimensional verification chapter by Sekisaka–Nii remains inaccessible; its possible uniqueness result is therefore unresolved, rather than evidence of absence.

Here (H) means the condition in [[goerisch-width]], §0: exactly one spectral **value**, of finite multiplicity, in a specified open interval. A certified count of one including multiplicity would suffice and would additionally prove simplicity. The interval can lie strictly inside an essential-spectrum gap. Excluding the intervals on either side of a small candidate enclosure, while proving existence inside it, still allows several eigenvalues inside that enclosure.

Search coverage: “eigenvalue exclosure/excluding perturbed periodic”, “internal-gap homotopy/count/isolation”, “line-defect mode counting”, “second-order spectrum multiplicity”, “dissipative barrier spectral gaps”, “verified Evans/rotation number”, and “guaranteed cluster separation”; Royal Society, SIAM, IMA, EMS, Springer, AMS, arXiv, HAL, KIT, Cardiff and author repositories.

Locations below refer to printed book/journal pages when available; otherwise they explicitly identify the manuscript or PDF pagination. Bibliographic links identify the sources, and theorem summaries come from the indicated full-text locations. FEM means finite element method; DtN means Dirichlet-to-Neumann; RT means Raviart–Thomas.

## What the methods actually supply

### 1. Verified exclusion in a periodic internal gap

K. Nagatou, M. Plum and M. T. Nakao, **“Eigenvalue Excluding for Perturbed-Periodic One-Dimensional Schrödinger Operators,”** *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* **468** (2012), 545–562, [doi:10.1098/rspa.2011.0159](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.2011.0159), [public full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/235982815_Eigenvalue_excluding_for_perturbed-periodic_one-dimensional_Schrodinger_operators).

**This is an actual verified exclusion computation on an unbounded periodic background.** Theorem 6.1, pp.558–559, combines the inequalities (6.11), (6.12), (6.16), a verified finite linear solve, and the strict component test (6.25). Success excludes eigenvalues throughout the tested parameter interval. Outside the essential spectrum, this gives a resolvent interval. Table 1, p.560, includes the verified interval $[8.822,10.585]$ for $a=3,c=1$; Table 2, pp.560–561, uses $N+1=400$ nodes and small parameter intervals. No elapsed-time figure was found there.

The unresolved candidate islands have no certified upper count. For our guide, the precise missing ingredient is a computable two-dimensional periodic-background resolvent and exterior-error bound. The authors identify this obstacle explicitly on p.548: their one-dimensional construction uses two fundamental solutions and an explicit Green function. A port-based replacement would need uniform bounds for all boundary data, followed by a separate count inside the candidate island.

### 2. The 2014 exclosure paper and its access limit

Y. Watanabe, K. Nagatou, M. Plum and M. T. Nakao, **“Verified Computations of Eigenvalue Exclosures for Eigenvalue Problems in Hilbert Spaces,”** *SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis* **52** (2014), 975–992, [doi:10.1137/120894683](https://doi.org/10.1137/120894683), [accessible first-page text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/264911351_Verified_Computations_of_Eigenvalue_Exclosures_for_Eigenvalue_Problems_in_Hilbert_Spaces).

**Its accessible formulation has a compact embedding.** The introduction on p.975 sets $D(A)\subset X\subset Y$, explicitly assumes $D(A)\hookrightarrow X$ compact, and states $(A+Q)u=\lambda Bu$ in (1.1). The full theorem statements were unavailable, so this survey assigns no broader applicability to them. In particular, a theorem for bounded-domain PDE exclosure would need an additional tail or DtN validation before it excluded spectrum of our strip. The related author/publication searches did not yield a full-text theorem implementing the required two-dimensional perturbed-periodic extension.

### 3. Homotopy really can certify isolation on an unbounded waveguide—but the worked problem is below the essential bottom

M. T. Nakao, M. Plum and Y. Watanabe, **Numerical Verification Methods and Computer-Assisted Proofs for Partial Differential Equations**, Springer Series in Computational Mathematics **53** (2019), [doi:10.1007/978-981-13-7669-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-13-7669-6); local `ref/ref_data/books/Nakao2019.pdf`.

**The book supplies a complete comparison-counting mechanism, with an explicit restriction relevant here.** Theorem 10.31, p.389, requires $\Lambda_N<\nu\le\lambda_{N+1}$ for the ordered eigenvalues below the essential bottom. Section 10.2.4 propagates this information from a comparison problem. The domain-decomposition theorem, Theorem 10.37, pp.400–404, requires the exterior coercivity inequality (10.127), p.400, at the chosen threshold. Our original operator has essential spectrum below the target, so the finite-index count and that exterior inequality at the target are unavailable.

Example 10.38, pp.404–406, goes further than inclusion alone: splitting a straight acoustic guide with a square obstacle gives **at most one** eigenvalue below $\pi^2/4$ in the selected symmetry sector; a trial Rayleigh quotient gives existence. Quadratic FEM and RT fluxes then enclose it. Table 10.2, p.406, reports $[1.7727,1.7765]$ with 23,296 elements and truncation length $L=10$. In that sector the essential spectrum is $[\pi^2/4,\infty)$, so this is a below-bottom certificate, rather than an internal periodic-band-gap example.

The original source is H. Behnke, U. Mertins, M. Plum and C. Wieners, **“Eigenvalue Inclusions via Domain Decomposition,”** *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* **456** (2000), 2717–2730, [published full text](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=d2ab751db1bec6ad996c3e78a8eb6cf6a57a9125&repid=rep1&type=pdf), [doi:10.1098/rspa.2000.0635](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.2000.0635). Its Theorem 2.1, pp.2719–2720, gives the flux-based inclusion; Lemma 3.1, p.2723, orders eigenvalues below the essential bottom; Lemma 4.1 and Table 1, p.2726, give the waveguide comparison. Section 4, p.2725, identifies verified inner products for the small inclusion matrices as the dominant cost. A spectral transformation or an exact bounded-core reduction is the smallest plausible change for an internal gap; changing the scalar shift alone does not remove the lower essential bands.

### 4. Exact counts already exist for weak periodic line defects

B. M. Brown, V. Hoang, M. Plum, M. Radosz and I. Wood, **“Gap Localization of Multiple TE-Modes by Arbitrarily Weak Defects,”** *Journal of the London Mathematical Society* **102** (2020), 773–795, [doi:10.1112/jlms.12337](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.12337). Read local `Brown2020.pdf`, arXiv:1901.05102v1, whose title is **“Gap Localization of TE-Modes by Arbitrarily Weak Defects—Multiband Case.”** [Public manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.05102).

**Theorem 3, manuscript p.16, gives exactly $n$ eigenvalues in the gap.** Here $n$ is the number of band-index/quasimomentum pairs attaining its upper edge. Theorem 1, p.13, supplies the essential upper count; Theorem 2, pp.15–16, supplies the lower count. The hypotheses include positive bounded material coefficients, a nonnegative nontrivial defect, nonconstant band functions, quadratic nondegeneracy at the relevant minima, and sufficiently small inverse-permittivity perturbation. This is the divergence-form TE operator on a fixed-quasimomentum strip with one periodic background on both sides. When $n=1$, (H) follows in this setting.

The result is analytic; its “sufficiently small” threshold is not a verified number for our configuration. Our weighted Laplacian, fixed defect contrast and potentially different leads require a different application argument. Computing the relevant band extrema and a quantitative admissible perturbation threshold would be the cost of turning this theorem into a certificate where its assumptions fit. It gives no measured certification cost for our guide.

The related weighted-Laplacian count is B. M. Brown, V. Hoang, M. Plum and I. Wood, **“Spectrum Created by Line Defects in Periodic Structures,”** *Mathematische Nachrichten* **287** (2014), 1972–1985, [doi:10.1002/mana.201300165](https://doi.org/10.1002/mana.201300165), [arXiv:1306.6256](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.6256). Theorem 3, preprint p.12, gives an exact count under its weak-positive-defect and band-edge assumptions in the three-dimensional line-defect setting. Thus an assertion that line-defect counts have never been proved would be wrong; the remaining issue is their quantitative applicability to this fixed guide.

### 5. Davies–Plum: a genuine internal-gap isolation example, and an explicit isolation hypothesis in the numerical refinement method

E. B. Davies and M. Plum, **“Spectral Pollution,”** *IMA Journal of Numerical Analysis* **24** (2004), 417–438, [doi:10.1093/imanum/24.3.417](https://doi.org/10.1093/imanum/24.3.417), [full manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0302145).

**Lemma 12, arXiv PDF p.17, proves isolation for a particular noncompact selfadjoint operator.** Compress multiplication by $b$ to the zero-mean subspace of $L^2(0,\pi)$. Isolated eigenvalues are simple roots of $\int_0^\pi b(x)/(b(x)-\lambda)\,dx=\pi$. For $b=\mathbf1_E$, $0<|E|<\pi$, there is exactly one isolated eigenvalue $(\pi-|E|)/\pi$ between the essential spectral points $0$ and $1$. This supplies an affirmative special-case answer with an analytic count at scalar cost.

The general enclosure refinement in §5 instead explicitly assumes their hypothesis (H), arXiv PDF p.10; Lemma 7, p.11, uses it. The equivalence with Zimmermann–Mertins bounds is Theorem 11, PDF pp.14–15. Our guide lacks the special scalar equation. Its counterpart would be a validated operator-valued boundary equation and a complete count, rather than a plot of a finite residual-distance function.

### 6. Lehmann–Goerisch and Zimmermann–Mertins do not themselves supply the missing upper count

S. Zimmermann and U. Mertins, **“Variational Bounds to Eigenvalues of Self-Adjoint Eigenvalue Problems with Arbitrary Spectrum,”** *Zeitschrift für Analysis und ihre Anwendungen* **14** (1995), 327–345, [doi:10.4171/ZAA/677](https://doi.org/10.4171/ZAA/677), [full text](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/34815).

**Their bounds work with essential spectrum present.** Theorem 1.1, p.330, uses local variational indices around a shift; Theorem 4.2, p.343, gives the corresponding one-sided approximations. Corollary 2.1, p.335, includes the auxiliary-element condition behind Goerisch-type bounds. These results establish the existence and one-sided locations of locally indexed eigenvalues. They do not bound from above how many additional eigenvalues lie in an unverified interval. The numerical example's separating order is described as a heuristic assumption on p.344.

Nakao et al., Remark 10.11(g), p.363, states this limitation explicitly for its general Lehmann theorem and points to Nagatou–Plum–Nakao for exclosure. The extension needed here is therefore an upper-count certificate, not another version of the same inclusion formula. Once that count is available, the current flux calculation can supply the narrow enclosure without repeating the count at its final accuracy.

### 7. Second-order relative spectra: safe inclusions, with a real condition behind multiplicity recovery

M. Levitin and E. Shargorodsky, **“Spectral Pollution and Second-Order Relative Spectra for Self-Adjoint Operators,”** *IMA Journal of Numerical Analysis* **24** (2004), 393–416, [doi:10.1093/imanum/24.3.393](https://doi.org/10.1093/imanum/24.3.393), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0212087). **Theorem 2.5, preprint p.9**, says a quadratic spectral point $z$ gives $[\operatorname{Re}z-|\operatorname{Im}z|,\operatorname{Re}z+|\operatorname{Im}z|]\cap\sigma(A)\ne\varnothing$. Its input is an operator-domain trial space. This is inclusion, not exclusion of additional real spectrum.

L. Boulton and M. Levitin, **“On Approximation of the Eigenvalues of Perturbed Periodic Schrödinger Operators,”** *Journal of Physics A* **40** (2007), 9319–9329, [doi:10.1088/1751-8113/40/31/010](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8113/40/31/010); local `Boulton2007.pdf`, [preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0702420). **Corollary 2.5, p.6**, gives inclusion for perturbed-periodic operators; **Corollary 2.6, p.7**, gives a quadratic improvement using already known isolation. **Theorem 2.4, p.6**, excludes quadratic spectral points from a disk when the corresponding real interval is already spectrum-free. Reversing that implication to certify an empty interval would be an error. Their one- and two-dimensional examples use operator-domain approximation; our conforming $H^1$ FEM field would need additional regularity treatment for this route.

L. Boulton and M. Strauss, **“On the Convergence of Second-Order Spectra and Multiplicity,”** *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* **467** (2011), 264–284, [doi:10.1098/rspa.2010.0233](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.2010.0233), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0774). **Theorem 7, pp.13–14**, recovers twice the exact algebraic multiplicity in the quadratic spectrum, provided the trial space approximates an orthonormal basis of the **whole** exact cluster in the stated graph norm and satisfies the explicit separation-dependent threshold. **Corollary 8, p.14**, gives limiting spectral exactness in an essential gap. A single computed conjugate pair alone supplies neither the whole-eigenspace approximation bound nor a count excluding missed eigenvectors. The required additional computation would control the spectral complement, which is also the missing ingredient in the direct DtN route.

### 8. Supercells and dissipative methods: convergence can preserve multiplicity without certifying a particular finite calculation

E. Cancès, V. Ehrlacher and Y. Maday, **“Non-consistent Approximations of Self-adjoint Eigenproblems: Application to the Supercell Method,”** *Numerische Mathematik* **128** (2014), 663–706; local `Cances2014.pdf`, [full preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.0331).

**Theorem 5.1, pp.14–15**, gives spectral exactness for $-\Delta+V_{\rm per}+W$ on $\mathbb R^d$, with decaying $W$, and equality of exact/approximate multiplicities for sufficiently large supercells. Its quantitative part starts with $\sigma(A)\cap(\lambda-\varepsilon,\lambda+\varepsilon)=\{\lambda\}$. **Theorem 5.2, p.16**, incorporates numerical integration under additional regularity. The constants and sufficiently-large threshold are not a finite, verified test establishing that initial isolation interval. Using compact-domain lower bounds on a supercell would therefore still require a certified transfer of the **count** to the infinite guide. Different periodic leads and discontinuous curved material coefficients also require changes to the stated model and integration analysis.

M. Marletta and R. Scheichl, **“Eigenvalues in Spectral Gaps of Differential Operators,”** *Journal of Spectral Theory* **2** (2012), 293–320, [doi:10.4171/JST/30](https://doi.org/10.4171/JST/30), [full text](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/33475). **Theorems 5–6, pp.301–302**, control truncation and spurious limits for the dissipatively perturbed half-line problem; **Theorem 10, p.306**, approximates an already isolated eigenvalue by a complex shifted one. Example 4, pp.313–314, computes a two-dimensional periodic-potential example. These results supply convergent approximations, but the stated constants and isolation premises do not give a validated upper count for our finite FEM calculation.

S. Aljawi and M. Marletta, **“On the Eigenvalues of Spectral Gaps of Elliptic PDEs on Waveguides,”** *Integral Equations and Operator Theory* **95** (2023), article 7, 1–40, [doi:10.1007/s00020-023-02727-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00020-023-02727-6), [accepted manuscript](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/158306/1/SAljawifinal.pdf). **Theorem 3.3, manuscript p.15**, gives exponential convergence of boundary maps on a half-waveguide for a complex parameter whose real part lies in a specified tail gap. **Theorem 4.12, pp.24–25**, transfers isolated eigenvalues of the dissipative operator under truncation; its proof uses regularized determinants and Rouché's theorem. This is a useful two-dimensional precursor to a count-transfer proof. A certificate for our original selfadjoint problem would need computable constants, FEM error bounds, and a verified contour or real-axis count; the theorem treats convergence to an eigenvalue already assumed isolated.

J. Hinchcliffe and M. Strauss, **“Spectral Enclosure and Superconvergence for Eigenvalues in Gaps,”** *Integral Equations and Operator Theory* **84** (2016), 1–32, [doi:10.1007/s00020-015-2247-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00020-015-2247-0), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.4066v3). **Theorems 4.5–4.6, pp.13–14, and Theorem 5.3, p.20**, establish eigenspace/eigenvalue convergence for finite-rank dissipative perturbations and their approximations. The rank equality in (5.4), p.16, is for sufficiently large approximation spaces. Quantities measuring approximation of the exact eigenspace enter the estimates. A computable bound proving the required size threshold would be needed to turn the observed finite rank into isolation.

### 9. Guaranteed lower bounds can certify separation after a suitable reduction

E. Cancès, G. Dusson, Y. Maday, B. Stamm and M. Vohralík, **“Guaranteed a Posteriori Bounds for Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors: Multiplicities and Clusters,”** *Mathematics of Computation* **89** (2020), 2563–2611, [doi:10.1090/mcom/3549](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3549); local `Cances2020.pdf`, [preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.04140).

**Remark 4.3, preprint p.14, really does make cluster separation checkable.** Equations (4.2)–(4.3) combine conforming upper bounds with guaranteed neighbouring lower bounds. For a one-eigenvalue cluster these prove the needed separation. The framework, §2.1, p.4, assumes compact resolvent. Our infinite strip has infinitely many spectral directions below the internal-gap target, so the same globally indexed finite list is unavailable. The natural reuse is on a rigorously reduced bounded-core operator, with an additional correspondence/count argument for the original nonlinear problem.

### 10. Certified lead gaps and exact DtN reduction are separate ingredients

V. Hoang, M. Plum and C. Wieners, **“A Computer-Assisted Proof for Photonic Band Gaps,”** *Zeitschrift für angewandte Mathematik und Physik* **60** (2009), 1035–1052, [doi:10.1007/s00033-008-8021-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00033-008-8021-2); local `Hoang2009a.pdf`.

**Theorem 7, p.1046, certifies a whole bulk gap from finitely many verified Bloch calculations.** Theorems 1–2, pp.1039–1040, give cell Ritz/Goerisch bounds, with the spectral comparison/homotopy in §4, pp.1042–1044. Explicit perturbation radii cover the Bloch parameter set. Figure 5, p.1049, reports 95 covering balls, primal dimension 12,290 and 52 hours for its full verification. This is historical cost for a two-parameter example, not a forecast for our fixed-transverse-momentum calculation. Applying the method separately to our two leads would verify a common essential gap after adapting the true-geometry integration. It leaves the defect eigenvalue count to another step.

S. Fliss, **“A Dirichlet-to-Neumann Approach for the Exact Computation of Guided Modes in Photonic Crystal Waveguides,”** *SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing* **35** (2013), B438–B461, [doi:10.1137/12086697X](https://doi.org/10.1137/12086697X); local `Fliss2013.pdf`, [preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1202.4928).

**Theorem 4.5, manuscript p.13, preserves the eigenvalue and its multiplicity under exact DtN reduction.** Proposition 4.8, p.14, gives compact resolvent for each frozen-parameter core operator; Proposition 4.10, p.16, characterizes guided modes by its nonlinear fixed-point equations. Theorem 4.1 and Remark 4.2, pp.10–11, identify half-lead Dirichlet poles and discuss Robin-to-Robin replacement. A small residual of the finite DtN matrix alone supplies no norm error for the exact boundary operator or upper count for the nonlinear spectrum.

S. Fliss, P. Joly and V. Lescarret, **“A Dirichlet-to-Neumann Approach to the Mathematical and Numerical Analysis in Waveguides with Periodic Outlets at Infinity,”** *Pure and Applied Analysis* **3** (2021), 487–526, [doi:10.2140/paa.2021.3.487](https://doi.org/10.2140/paa.2021.3.487); local `Fliss2021.pdf`, [HAL manuscript](https://inria.hal.science/hal-02444754v1). **Theorem 4.8, manuscript p.23 / PDF p.24**, characterizes the unique decaying propagation operator by the Riccati equation and spectral radius below one at a nonpropagating frequency, subject also to the stated threshold, edge and cell exclusions. Its continuous characterization is useful for validating each periodic port. It gives no verified discretization bound for our port matrices. Handling the two different leads independently is an adaptation of this construction, not a completed isolation computation.

## Recommended route for this guide

**Use exact DtN reduction and a verified local count on the bounded core.** This keeps the direct FEM trial and flux enclosure intact and confines the expensive spectral work to establishing a reasonably sized isolation interval. The following is a proposed certification program, not an application already completed in the cited papers.

1. **Certify an essential-spectrum-free window.** Apply the Hoang–Plum–Wieners cell-bound/covering construction to the longitudinal Bloch parameter of each lead at the fixed transverse phase. Intersect the two certified windows. Check the operator comparison that identifies the joined guide's essential spectrum with the two limiting lead spectra. Only a window containing the desired isolation interval is needed; exact band-edge locations are unnecessary.
2. **Validate the exact ports on that window.** Bound each continuous DtN map in the $H^{1/2}\to H^{-1/2}$ trace norm, including the omitted trace modes, true curved-interface integration and errors in the cell/propagation solves. Establish that the window contains no half-lead Dirichlet pole for the chosen cuts. Fliss supplies the continuous reduction and propagation characterization; explicit, computable port-error and pole-exclusion bounds are work still required for our model. If a cut has a pole, its contribution must be resolved or a pole-free boundary formulation used before counting.
3. **Turn the nonlinear problem into a local crossing count.** On the fixed core form domain define
   $$t_\lambda(u,u)=\int_{D}|\nabla u|^2-\lambda\int_D\rho|u|^2+\sum_{\pm}\langle\Lambda_\pm(\lambda)u|_{\Gamma_\pm},u|_{\Gamma_\pm}\rangle.$$
   The proposed real-axis argument uses the derivative of this form with fixed core field: it equals minus the total weighted mass of the core field and its exact decaying lead extensions. Establishing this identity, its regularity and the absence of poles gives strictly downward zero crossings. Together with multiplicity preservation and the frozen-parameter Gårding bound, this should identify the number in $(\alpha_-,\alpha_+)$ with the increase of the finite negative index of $t_\lambda$ between the endpoints. This count theorem for the validated two-lead formulation must be supplied; it is not a claim that Fliss already certified the count.
4. **Certify both endpoint indices, including the infinite complement.** For each endpoint, split the core form into a finite FEM block and its complement. Prove positivity of the complementary block and enclose the finite Schur-complement inertia, including port errors. Alternatively, guaranteed neighbouring eigenvalue bounds for the frozen core could determine the same index after adapting their hypotheses to its nonlocal boundary form. The cited lower-bound and Schur-complement papers supply templates; their constants for local compact problems cannot simply be reused for the DtN form. Verify invertibility at both endpoints. An index increase of one proves one simple eigenvalue and hence (H).
5. **Then compute the narrow interval.** Once the count is certified, apply the existing Lehmann–Goerisch enclosure with enclosed integrals and finite arithmetic. Prop.5 can already provide existence inside the certified essential gap while the upper-count work proceeds. Neither existence nor a successful local normalized-eigenpair Newton test alone replaces the spectral-complement part of step 4.

The cost drivers are verified cell solves across the lead parameter covering, continuous port/complement error bounds, and two endpoint inertia computations. They can be much more expensive than the final scalar Goerisch formula. Using a proper subinterval avoids proving a count throughout the entire gap, but the chosen endpoint distance also controls the final enclosure constant. No reliable runtime estimate for this certification is available from the current floating-point runs.

**One alternative: fold the spectrum.** Boulton–Levitin, Remark 2.3, preprint p.5, observes that $(A-s)^2$ moves eigenvalues near $s$ below its essential bottom. Choose $s$ and $r$ so $[s-r,s+r]$ lies inside a certified essential gap; a certified count of exactly one eigenvalue of $(A-s)^2$ below $r^2$ would prove (H) for the corresponding interval of $A$. This permits below-bottom comparison ideas, but requires a fourth-order/graph-domain formulation, rigorous exterior estimates and an upper count for the folded operator. Squaring the finite FEM matrix alone omits those requirements. The DtN route is preferable here because it retains the existing second-order core and port formulation.

## Short answers to S1 and S2

**S1 — Prop.5 is a Weinstein-type spectral inclusion in transformed variables.** The closest stated theorem is D. Weinstein's result as Theorem 10.6 in Nakao–Plum–Watanabe, p.356: a normalized operator-domain trial with residual at most $\varepsilon$ gives a spectrum-hitting interval of radius $\varepsilon$, without isolation. The following identification is a deduction, not a quotation of a published $q,J$ formula. Put $C=A+\gamma$ and $B_q=C^{1/2}-qC^{-1/2}$; its domain is the form domain of $A$. For the normalized trial in [[goerisch-width]], $\|B_qv\|_\rho^2=q(qG-1)\le q(qJ-1)$. Apply Weinstein to $B_q$ at zero and pull back through $x\mapsto\sqrt{x}-q/\sqrt{x}$. This gives exactly Prop.5's endpoints and width $q\sqrt{\delta(4+\delta)}$, $\delta=qJ-1$. No inspected source prints this precise flux-majorized formula; the underlying first-order spectrum-inclusion principle is already known.

**S2 — No inspected source applies the whole FEM-trial/computed-$H(\mathrm{div})$-flux enclosure to a periodic line-defect eigenvalue in an internal gap.** The closest actual waveguide computation is Behnke–Mertins–Plum–Wieners 2000, Theorem 2.1 and §4(a), pp.2719–2720 and 2725–2726, reproduced in Nakao et al., Example 10.38: FEM, RT flux, comparison and rigorous inclusion are all present, in a symmetry sector below its essential bottom. Hoang–Plum–Wieners 2009 is closer to our dielectric material model, but certifies bulk Bloch-cell bands rather than a defect mode. Brown–Hoang–Plum–Wood's **“On Spectral Bounds for Photonic Crystal Waveguides,”** in *Inequalities and Applications*, ISNM **157** (2009), 23–30, [doi:10.1007/978-3-7643-8773-0_3](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-7643-8773-0_3), Eqs.(5)–(6), pp.25–26, gives a first-order spectral inclusion using a specially constructed strip trial and residual majorization; it does not supply the direct FEM-plus-flux second-order isolation certificate sought here.
